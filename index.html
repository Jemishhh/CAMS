Read it as a loop, not a chain: each turn makes the next turn cheaper. Because CAMS' incremental cost per folio is near-zero, the same AUM growth produces higher margins each cycle — which funds platform investment, which lowers the AMC's cost to outsource, which pulls more assets onto the platform. Cost growth ~9% vs revenue growth ~13% is this loop showing up in the P&L.

Of every ₹10,000 of mutual fund assets CAMS keeps records for, it takes home about ₹2.40 a year.

The fee is invisible to the investor — the AMC pays it out of its own expense ratio. That is what makes it sticky, and what caps it.

The AMC is the customer, not the investor. CAMS is hired to run the register: it opens accounts, processes the purchase, the redemption, the switch and the SIP debit, computes distributor brokerage, answers the phone at 285 service centres, and files what the regulator asks for.

Quantified risk → mitigant

Revenue-yield compression
Yield has fallen ~0.065 bps/yr (2.88 → 2.75 across FY26–FY28E) — gradual repricing, not a cliff. Non-MF revenue (14% of the total) carries no yield exposure and grows faster.
—
AUM is market-linked
~43% of industry AUM arrives via SIP, which is contractual and keeps arriving through a drawdown. This is the risk with no operational mitigant — only the SIP mix softens it.
—
Market-share loss to KFin
A two-player market. Switching provider means migrating the entire investor record book, reconciliation history and regulatory reporting under SEBI supervision — an operational risk for the AMC, not a procurement decision.
—
Multiple de-rating
The default 36x exit already de-rates from today's 40.2x trailing and sits below the 38.2x 5-yr mean. A fall to the −1SD level of 29.7x is the realistic bear.
—
Client concentration
31 MF RTA clients, but assets skew heavily to the largest AMCs — the three biggest CAMS serves (SBI, ICICI Pru, HDFC) hold ~₹27.6 L Cr, roughly half the AUM it services.
